### Notes per update

**May 2026 core update** — launched 21 May 2026, completed 2 Jun 2026. Google's
framing: a regular update "designed to better surface relevant, satisfying
content for searchers from all types of sites." Google published **no new
guidance** alongside it; the standing core-update advice applies. Second core
update of 2026, and widely reported as more volatile than March.

**June 2026 spam update** — launched 24 Jun 2026. **No new spam policies**;
refinement of existing detection. Google confirmed to press that it did **not**
target link spam and did **not** target the site reputation abuse policy, which
points it at content-level violations — scaled content abuse above all, plus
doorways, cloaking, keyword stuffing. It does **not** target AI content for
being AI-made; it targets content with no value, which is often but not always
AI-generated.

**August 2026 spam update** — announced on the Search Status Dashboard and via
Google Search Central on X/LinkedIn, 18 Aug 2026 ≈12:30 pm ET, finished 21 Aug
2026 ≈4:50 am ET. Google's wording: *"This is a normal spam update, and it will
roll out for all languages and locations. The rollout may take a few days to
complete."* No new policies, no companion blog post. Confirmed as **not**
targeting link spam specifically. Third spam update of 2026.

## Spam policy wording verified 2026-09-01

Read directly from
<https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies> while
answering the location-page question (SKILL.md §8).

**Doorway abuse** — creating multiple pages or sites "to rank for specific,
similar search queries" that funnel users through "intermediate pages that
aren't as useful as the final destination". Examples:

- "Having multiple websites with slight variations to the URL and home page to
  maximize their reach for any specific query"
- "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities
  that funnel users to one page"
- "Generating pages to funnel visitors into the actual usable or relevant
  portion of a site"
- "Creating substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a
  clearly defined, browseable hierarchy"

**Scaled content abuse** — "many pages generated for the primary purpose of
manipulating search rankings and not helping users". Examples:

- "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages
  without adding value for users"
- "Scraping feeds, search results, or other content to generate many pages …
  where little value is provided to users"
- "Stitching or combining content from different web pages without adding value"
- "Creating multiple sites with the intent of hiding the scaled nature of the
  content"

**Scam and fraud** — covers "impersonating an official business or service" and
"intentionally displaying false information about a business or service",
including impersonation "to trick users into paying money to the wrong party".

## Claims deliberately recorded as *false or unsupported*

Keep these straight; they come up constantly.

- **"E-E-A-T is a ranking factor / we have an E-E-A-T score."** No. The SEO
  Starter Guide's own myth table marks E-E-A-T as **not** a ranking factor.
  Google's ranking systems use signals that *align with* the concept; the
  concept itself is rater vocabulary.
- **"Quality rater scores affect my rankings."** No — rater data is feedback
  used to evaluate systems, not a direct ranking input.
- **"Topical authority is a Google ranking system."** No. Industry vocabulary
  for relevance + coverage. No score exists.
- **"Google penalises duplicate content."** No such penalty. Copying *other
  people's* content is a separate problem (scraping).
- **"Google penalises AI-written content."** No. It acts on *scaled content
  abuse* — mass-produced pages with no added value — regardless of tool.
- **"You need llms.txt / AI schema to appear in AI Overviews."** Google's own
  AI-features doc says no new files, no special structured data, no special
  optimisation.
- **"Keyword density / word count / meta keywords / heading order."** Not
  ranking factors.
